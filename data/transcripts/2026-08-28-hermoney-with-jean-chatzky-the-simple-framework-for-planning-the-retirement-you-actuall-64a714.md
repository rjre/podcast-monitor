So how do you go about actually deciding where those hours should go? In the book, I walk through two frameworks that I use with people to help them make these sorts of choices. The first one I call five W's and an H. Before you start putting any of this down in writing, ask yourself six questions to help yourself frame your retirement wants. Number one, who is actually retiring? Is it just you, you and a partner? And if so, are the two of you picturing the same life? Two, when do you plan on making the change? Three, what do you want to do with your time when you get there? Four, where do you want to be or live during this next phase? Five, why are you making this change in the first place? And six, how are you feeling about all of it? Excited? Nervous? Maybe both? These are big questions and they deserve more than one more to answer. So feel free to think about them, write some thoughts down, noodle them and come back and do it again. The second framework in my book borrows from design thinking, the same process that product designers use, but applied to your own life. Step one is to empathize with yourself. Think about what actually brings you joy, what feels meaningful or purposeful to you as far as retirement is concerned. Try to get away from what you think retirement is supposed to look like. Step two, take your answers from step one and turn them into an actual written retirement goal. Step three is to ideate, get really specific about the things that you want to do in the short term, in the medium term, and in the long term. Step four, prototype. Take a few of those ideas and actually try them on. Try them out before you commit to them fully.
