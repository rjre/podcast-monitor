When I introduced myself to you back in 2020, I told you about getting out of 30 grand of debt in 11 months, saving 60% of my income, walking the Camino de Santiago, buying a house and building the economy conference. I was full of momentum and optimism and a little bit of hustle energy. I was someone who had figured out the mechanics of personal finance and was just starting to notice the first hints of what financial security might actually feel like. A lot has changed since then. Economy is now a profitable, sold-out, annual event that I run on roughly two months of full-time work per year. I left my corporate job in January of 2021. I got married to my midwestern gentleman and my relationship with money has shifted in ways I didn't fully anticipate when I started hosting this show. Back in 2020, I was pretty hyper-vigilant about money. I was budgeting, tracking, optimizing and fixated on reaching my financial goals. Those habits were really important. They kick-started me on a promising trajectory. But somewhere along the way something shifted. The vigilance softened into trust. The scarcity mindset loosened into something that feels a lot more like ease. I am not yet financially independent by the traditional definition. I'm doing the coast-fives, slow-fi version of this journey. But the pursuit itself has led me to create a life I don't feel like I need to retire from. And I've come to learn that intentional living is harder and richer than any spreadsheet can capture. What's crazy to me is that the pursuit of a financial goal became a vehicle for becoming more fully myself. And I think that's what the path to financial independence actually does when you do it intentionally. At least that's what happened for me.
