Five health tips for entrepreneurs to make generational health part of your generational wealth. Tip number one, don't work over time. Decide on a time when you stop working no matter what's going on, so your body and your mind can wind down for the day and prepare for a good night's sleep. In my experience, most people who struggle with insomnia have troubles shutting their mental chatter down. When you incorporate a healthy sleep routine, which includes being able to stop working at a certain time for most days, then your nervous system can calm down and you can fall asleep and stay asleep. Tip number two, don't skip meals. Especially lunch. At lunch time, our digestive system is primed to properly digest our foods so we can properly absorb the necessary nutrients and eliminate waste. Furthermore, when we skip lunch, we have a tendency to overeat at dinner and eat dinner late. Tip number three, walk for 15 minutes every morning. The first thing I always encourage my patients to do is walk in the morning for 15 minutes every single day. When you do something every day, it creates consistency and continuity, which are the bed rocks of a healthy lifestyle. Inevitably, within three months, my patients are walking for 30 to 45 minutes without any effort. Their heart health is improved, their weight is down, and they have a tremendous amount of energy. All you have to do is start small and keep going. Tip number four, make time for creativity. Being an entrepreneur is not just about doing business. You need to set time aside to get new ideas. This time is essential. It should be part of your weekly schedule, just as replying to customers' calls or spending time for marketing are. So, spend some time alone in nature preferably and allow inspiration to come to you. And tip number five, take a regular break from being an entrepreneur. This one is a tough one because we are so conditioned to believe that if we don't spend every single minute of our day on our business, then we're losing business. And that is simply not true. What we're losing, however, is ourselves. We are not our business, no matter how much we love it. We are human beings, not just human doings, and we need time to just be.
