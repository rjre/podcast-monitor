This summer seems especially challenging with inflation, high gas prices, and sky high lodging in desirable locations. If inflation is keeping you from planning a trip this summer, I want you to consider five hacks that can save a lot of money and still mean spending time with your family. So let's get into them. Hack number one, discover destinations close to home. One of the easiest ways to spend less on a getaway is visiting a destination that's relatively close, like within a 50 or 60-mile radius where you live. So challenge yourself to be a tourist in your own area and visit places you haven't been to for, or maybe places you have been and know you'd enjoy going again. An adventure doesn't have to be about traveling a long distance. It can be about seeing something new with your family. You might take a day trip to a federal or state park, a historical town, a museum, or a nearby lake, beach, or wildlife area. If you choose nearby attractions that are free or low-cost, you can have a full day and then come home instead of paying for lodging or taking that full-blown trip to Maui. Or you might find a rental or a hotel at a reasonable price to stay overnight if you're not visiting a famous or popular tourist destination. Hack number two, take a low-cost road trip. Road trips are a family tradition that creates lots of great memories. Yes, gas prices are higher right now. However, there are ways to minimize what you pay at the pump. You'll certainly save money compared to booking flights to a destination and then needing a rental car to get around. Before you leave home, doing a little planning can help you save money and have more fun on the road. Carefully plan your route to reduce unnecessary miles, avoid rush hour traffic in major cities, and avoid gas stations right next to major highway interchanges, which almost always charge higher prices. For instance, you might find gas costs less by fueling up 15 miles before or after a state or city border. You can use an app like Gas Buddy to search for the cheapest fuel by city, state, brand, or even find charging stations for electric vehicles. Also consider paying for fuel with a credit card that offers rewards for gas stations, such as up to 5% cash back. Also check your tire pressure, get necessary oil changes, and complete any deferred maintenance ahead of a road trip. By keeping your vehicle properly maintained, you can boost fuel efficiency and you'll feel safer on the road. Another great part about a road trip is being able to pack plenty of food, drinks, and snacks to avoid paying higher prices at restaurants and convenience stores. You can buy the essentials like sunscreen, towels, coolers, snacks, and toys before your trip from a value retailer like Dollar General. They have thousands of items priced at a dollar or less every day, including food, snacks, and personal care items. They also offer a $5 off $25 digital coupon on Saturdays, and that's on top of already low prices. If you have a favorite discount store, download their app, which may offer extra savings. For instance, Dollar General's DG app has digital coupons, cash back, and personalized deals to maximize savings. So the trick to saving when traveling is staying in control of where you spend money, so you're never forced to overpay in a pinch. Hack number three. Spend less on lodging and food. If you want a longer summer getaway, consider booking it for the end of August or the beginning of September, that's when lodging prices typically drop. And if you have kids in school waiting until the end of the season, can cut about 30% or more off the cost of a rental or hotel. While vacation rentals may have hidden fees to watch for, like a cleaning fee and occupancy taxes, they can save you money on food costs. For instance, if you eat in or pack breakfast and lunch to take with you, you might only need to dine out for dinner as part of your vacation experience. That could easily save a family $100 or more daily. On the flip side, if you plan to eat out for every meal, staying in a reasonably priced hotel may cost less. So do your homework to compare the total night cost of various options that fit your needs and budget. Remember that most hotels are more expensive on weekends, so timing your stay for mid-week can save 30 to 50% off a room rates. If you plan to visit a federal or state park, some have cabins or lodging for a fraction of Airbnb or hotel prices, I've stated some of them and they're great. Some even have full kitchens, allowing you to completely control your food costs. Or if you enjoy camping and you've already invested it all the necessary year, that could be part of an affordable family adventure. Hack number 4. Take advantage of free and discounted entertainment. Once you have a plan to travel to a destination, consider the cost of entertainment. Of course, there are many public, lakes, beaches and parks that are always free. Also, check a town's online calendar or their public library website for municipal events. They could include free concerts, movies on the park, or nature workshops that welcome out-of-town visitors. When you want to go somewhere that charges admission, maybe like a water park, a theme park, zoo or aquarium, ticket prices can add up. So visit an organization's website to see if you qualify for any discounts or reciprocal memberships. For instance, while the American Automobile Association or AAA is famous for fixing flat tires and is certainly a great membership to have on a road trip, it's also one of the country's largest discount networks. Being a AAA member for about $65 a year gives you savings of up to 30% on tickets to major attractions, well-known theme parks and local sightseeing. Another incredible hack is that the American Association of Retired Persons or AARP is available no matter your age. You don't have to be retired. In a full membership, only cost about $15 a year and it gives significant discounts at theme parks, shows, events and movies. That can easily cover the cost of membership on your first ticket purchase. The National Park System offers an annual pass for $80 that gives you and everyone in your vehicle access to more than 2,000 federal recreation sites nationwide. They include national parks, forests and wildlife refuges. Getting a pass is a must if you visit parks with heavy entrance and parking fees like Grand Canyon or Yusemite. And hack five, visit low-cost destinations. For a budget-friendly vacation, look for destinations with reasonable lodging costs and free activities. For instance, the public beaches and golf shores Alabama are a hidden gem. I can tell you from experience that the sugar, white sand and the ocean are just as beautiful as the more famous or upscale neighboring beaches but the lodging costs less. I've been there, I know. I've also hiked and done bird-watching in the peaceful, bonsecure, national wildlife refuge for free. Main Augustine, Florida, which is America's oldest, continuously inhabited city, is another affordable destination I highly recommend, I've been there multiple times. You can combine historic sites seeing with a beach vacation. You can wander through the city, stunning Spanish colonial architecture and the pedestrian streets for free. Even St. Augustine's massive 17th century stone fortress on the water that Castillo de Marcos cost just a few dollars to tour. Washington, D.C. may not seem like a budget-friendly place because it is a big city but it has an incredible amount of free attractions. For instance, all 17 of the Smithsonian museums, the National Monuments and the Library of Congress cost nothing to visit. You might stay just outside the city to cut the cost of lodging and take the Metro subway into the city. The takeaway here is that you should not let the tourism industry dictate how you make memories with your family during the summer or at any time of the year.
